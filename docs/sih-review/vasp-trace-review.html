How tracing works

  • Algorithm: breadth-first over a frontier of branches from the seed. Each hop:
    1. check each branch head against the mixer list, then the curated/GraphSense labels, then labels harvested this trace;
    2. a hit ends the branch as a terminal;
    3. otherwise fetch the address, follow one asset (dominant by decimal amount), collapse to one edge per destination, and keep destinations carrying ≥10% of the outflow (always the biggest), at most 3 per parent and 8 alive overall.
  • Limits: 12 hops by default (API cap 20), 60 address lookups per trace.
  • Stops at: mixer hit · label hit · the address’s own tag · dead end · hop limit · budget exhausted. The last three are reported.
  • “Nearest exchange” means the largest share of the money that reached a named entity. Candidates on the same entity are merged; they are sorted by value share, then confidence; candidate #0 is the verdict, with its hop count shown.
  • Deposit address: the hop before an exchange terminal is scored by the sweep heuristic, using the transactions already fetched.
  • Weaknesses:
    • no time ordering;
    • fixed recency windows (Tron latest 600; Ethereum oldest 2,000);
    • labels without a category default to “exchange”;
    • value share uses all-time outflows, not a taint model;
    • fake tokens can steer which asset is followed.

Two semantic notes on the Indian CSV:

  • “CoinSwitch Binance Deposit 1” and “WazirX Binance Deposit 1” are Binance deposit addresses belonging to those exchanges’ Binance accounts. Labelling them only as the Indian exchange hides that Binance is also a disclosure target.
  • The CoinDCX and WazirX “Gas Supplier” addresses are operational wallets, ideal seeds for a gas-funding heuristic.

Clustering

There is no clustering algorithm: no common-input ownership, no change detection (on BTC the followed “dominant output” is often the change), no cluster expansion from deposit→hot-wallet sweeps. What exists is label pass-through and the per-hop deposit classifier.

Mixers, bridges, swaps

Mixers: one curated address, plus GraphSense mixing_service / coinjoin (mostly BTC); the Tornado ETH pools are typed “exchange”. Bridges and swap services: none. The only nod is “swap”/“dex” tags folded into defi.

Confidence and risk

Confidence = clamp(0.9 − 0.15 × hops + 0.05 if cluster-definer, 0.3, 0.95), so a single-source label at hop 0 shows 95%. Deposit score is a weighted checklist (0.35 / 0.30 / 0.20 / 0.15), threshold 0.60. Risk runs six ordered trail rules plus five behaviour typologies; the worse wins, the basis is named, and an exchange’s own wallet is not escalated.

Reports

The s.63 zip holds a canonical JSON record (no clock, sorted keys), Part A and Part B PDFs following the BSA Schedule with sworn fields left blank, and a hash report with certutil / sha256sum / shasum steps. Missing: a readable case report and a rendered notice letter. The certificate endpoint re-runs the trace, so after the 60 s cache it can differ from the screen.

SAHYOG mock

POST /v1/cases/{id}/sahyog regenerates the draft and resets status to draft. PATCH /v1/cases/{id}/sahyog sets status along draft → ready_for_submission → submitted → acknowledged → fulfilled. POST /v1/trace also returns a sahyog_draft. Draft payload:

Gaps: no request type (disclosure/freeze), no target legal entity, no officer or legal-order fields, no deposit address field (the key identifier), no transaction hashes.

Cases, dashboard, auth, audit

Cases hold open/pending/closed status, tags, NCRP ack, CCTNS FIR, a timeline and UPI hops. The dashboard shows six counts over the last 200 cases. Login is real; roles are cosmetic; every officer sees every case. The audit log exists but is not hash-chained. CORS * and the JWT in localStorage are disclosed simplifications.

Hardcoded vs live

Every trace, label hit, typology and exposure figure is computed live from explorer data. Hardcoded: the curated datasets, the Coverage page text and the Streamlit demo dropdown. There are no fake cases or seeded results; say so on stage.

Description

VASP-Trace is an India-first blockchain-intelligence engine for I4C and state cyber cells. Given a suspect wallet from an NCRP complaint, it follows the money across Tron, Ethereum, Polygon, BNB Chain, Solana and Bitcoin to the nearest exchange or custodial service. It names the specific deposit address that maps to one KYC’d account, and explains its confidence from named evidence. It scores the wallet’s own behaviour against published laundering typologies and screens every hop against sanctions lists. Then it produces what an Indian investigation needs next: a BNSS notice routed to the right VASP by FIU-IND registration status, and a Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam s.63 certificate with a reproducible hash. It runs on free public data, keeps case data in India, and says “unattributed” instead of guessing.

Elevator pitch

Paste a scam wallet and get the exchange deposit account behind it in seconds, with the evidence, the confidence and the right BNSS notice already drafted. VASP-Trace turns days of manual blockchain tracing into a court-ready SAHYOG request for India’s cyber police.

The 10 toughest jury questions

01SAHYOG has no public API. What did you integrate?

Public SAHYOG documentation describes takedown notices under IT Act s.79(3)(b); the Karnataka HC record lists 28+ issuing authorities. Your PS says VASP disclosure requests also flow through it. We built the engine side: a signed request pack, an adapter, and a mock SAHYOG speaking a proposed OpenAPI contract, all labelled MOCK. Connecting to the real portal is configuration once I4C exposes an endpoint.

02How do you know an attribution is right? What’s your false-positive rate?

Every attribution carries its evidence: label source and tier, hops, and deposit signals with their numbers. Unnamed addresses stay “unattributed”. Precision is not yet measured; next we evaluate against known exchange deposit addresses. The deposit heuristic is Victor (FC 2020), which clustered 17.9% of active Ethereum addresses.

03Launderers use 20 hops, mixers and bridges. Then what?

12 hops by default (20 max), a 60-lookup budget, every branch with ≥10% of the value kept, and dropped branches reported. A mixer ends the branch with HIGH exposure. A bridge is a typed terminal: we decode the destination when the protocol publishes it (THORChain memo, LI.FI, LayerZero); otherwise we say the trail continues on chain X.

04Most Indian fraud money is USDT on Tron. Can you freeze it?

Two paths:

  • at the exchange: s.94 for KYC, and a freeze under the procedure the jurisdiction’s High Court requires;
  • at the issuer: USDT has a contract blacklist (addBlackList / isBlackListed), so while funds sit in USDT we prepare an issuer-freeze request and show whether the address is already frozen.

05Which provision does your notice cite after the BNSS?

Disclosure: BNSS s.94. Freezing: the 2026 rulings split.

  • Andhra Pradesh HC (31 Mar 2026): a freeze needs a Magistrate’s order under s.107.
  • Karnataka HC (10 Aug 2026): a debit freeze is s.106 seizure, reported forthwith.
  • Bombay HC (30 Jul 2026): s.94 can’t freeze; ss.106–107 can.

The tool shows the split, drafts both variants, and the officer chooses. It never auto-files.

06Is the output admissible? Who signs?

BSA s.63 Part A (party) and Part B (expert) in the Schedule’s format, with a SHA-256 hash report. Sworn fields are left blank. The certified file is canonical JSON with no clock readings, so the defence can re-derive the hash; we are adding a block cutoff and raw-response hashes.

07Data sovereignty and security?

Case data stays in our self-hostable Postgres. Only public-chain lookups leave the system, and in production those move to I4C-hosted indexers. JWT auth and an audit log of every trace, certificate and status change are in place; roles and supervisor approval are being enforced.

08Why not just buy Chainalysis or TRM?

Use them if you have them; we are complementary. We add Indian VASP labels (the open datasets carry almost none), BNSS/BSA outputs, NCRP/CCTNS/UPI linkage, SAHYOG-shaped requests, and a cost that reaches every district cyber cell. Vendor labels can plug in as another source.

09How does it scale to lakhs of complaints?

A trace is bounded at 60 lookups. Today that is about 280–550 worst-case ETH traces a day per free key. Production means self-hosted indexers, a Redis queue with workers, a shared label store and a national cross-case graph. Linking reduces work: one notice covers many complaints.

10What about offshore and unregistered exchanges?

Routing is by registration:

  • registered Indian VASPs (CoinDCX, WazirX, CoinSwitch, ZebPay, per the Dec 2023 Lok Sabha reply) → direct notice;
  • registered offshore (Binance, KuCoin, Bybit) → their LE portals;
  • offshore entities in FIU-IND’s three waves (less those since registered) → LE portal, else a letter of request / MLAT, plus FIU-IND intimation;
  • stablecoin issuers → contract-level freeze.

Backup questions to rehearse: “Why is a single-source label 95%?” → fixed (defect 5). “Tornado sanctioned or not?” → delisted 21 Mar 2025. “Fake USDT?” → allow-list.
